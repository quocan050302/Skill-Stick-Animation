# Fact-Check Sources: "How Mine Salting Worked"

Historical and technical grounding verified via live web search (`2026-11-19`) across primary geological survey, mineralogical reference, and historical archive sources:

1. **Arizona Geological Survey (AZGS) — Mining Scams** (`azgs.arizona.edu/mineral-resources/mining-scams`):
   - Documents *mine salting* as the fraudulent introduction of valuable minerals (gold dust, nuggets, silver/rich ore) into barren rock, sample bags, or mining faces to deceive prospective investors and buyers before assay valuation.
2. **Mindat Glossary — Salting a Mine** (`mindat.org/glossary/salting_a_mine`):
   - Defines salting a mine / claim / ore sample as planting rich ore or gold artificially into a mine shaft, rock face, or assay sample so buyers overestimate deposit grade. Notes that modern geological random-sampling and independent certified assaying protocols evolved largely to detect salting.
3. **American Heritage — How To Salt A Gold Mine** (`americanheritage.com/how-salt-gold-mine`):
   - Documents classic frontier salting techniques, including contaminating small rock samples prior to assay inspection, firing gold-loaded charges into barren faces, or seeding sample pouches so isolated assays look rich while the bulk rock remains worthless.

## Editorial & Historical Discipline Applied to Script
- Presented strictly as an **illustrative composite historical explanation** of the technique (no fabricated specific scammer names, unverified dollar figures, exact dates, or invented quotes).
- Core causal claim stays strictly faithful to geological evidence: doctoring an isolated sample can make an assay spike temporarily, whereas independent multi-point samples taken directly from the surrounding host rock expose a barren claim ("The gold was real. The mine wasn't.").
